# Motion Art Direction

Treat motion as part of visual identity, not a bag of effects.

## Workflow

1. Define the emotional target in 3–5 words.
2. Choose a motion palette: calm, elastic, mechanical, floaty, snappy, organic, etc.
3. Assign motion hierarchy: hero, interaction, ambient, background.
4. Write a short beat sheet for the most important moments.
5. Decide what should remain still. Stillness gives motion contrast.

## Rules

- Reuse a small family of easing and timing behaviors.
- Strong motion belongs to meaningful state changes, not decoration.
- Ambient motion should usually be slower, smaller, and less frequent than reactive motion.
- Stagger related elements instead of synchronizing everything.
- Preserve spatial continuity. Objects should feel like they move from a place rather than teleport between layouts.
- Use scale, overlap, blur, contrast, and parallax consistently to support depth.
- Avoid animation simply because a library makes it easy.

## Deliverable expectation

Before implementation, state the primary motion character, dominant timing ranges, hero moments, interaction feedback language, ambient motion rules, and reduced-motion interpretation.
